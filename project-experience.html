These case studies describe my work in public safety and Marine Corps communications. They are separate from the fictional support demonstrations elsewhere in this portfolio.

Jacksonville | Firehouse to ESO equipment migration

Context: Jacksonville Fire and Emergency Services moved equipment reporting from Firehouse to ESO. The inventory included protective clothing, helmets, gloves, chainsaws, extrication equipment, ropes, and other operational assets.

My role: As Protective Equipment Manager, I was a principal driving force behind moving the equipment records into the new application.

What I did: Over several months, I reviewed Excel source documents and individually created more than 2,700 equipment records in ESO. This required sustained attention to individual items and follow-through across a substantial inventory.

Result: My work moved those equipment records into the new application.

Relevance to customer support: Understanding how operational users rely on records helps me ask specific questions about missing information, explain a new workflow, and maintain ownership through a lengthy transition.

Piney Green | Emergency Reporting to First Due

Context: Piney Green Volunteer Fire Department transitioned from Emergency Reporting to First Due in 2025.

My contribution: I assisted with implementation and helped migrate more than 1,500 hydrant records, including each hydrant's associated history.

Relevance to customer support: A record's history can be as important as its current fields. The experience reinforces the need to clarify what a user expects to retain when moving systems and to understand the purpose behind the information.

Jacksonville | Mobile-device adoption

Context: In June 2026, the department transitioned from Windows mobile computer terminals to approximately 24 iPads across 12 apparatus and four stations.

My contribution: I assisted with implementation, troubleshooting, and user adoption as personnel began using the new field technology.

Relevance to customer support: Supporting a change in the field requires explaining practical steps, listening to the user's immediate task, and coordinating specialized assistance when necessary.

Jacksonville Police | Operating procedures

During a temporary assignment to the telecommunications center in 2025, I authored or revised six SOPs. Topics included GlobalProtect VPN access, VIPER radio channels, on-call notifications, regulated messages, recovered vehicles, and communications-center conduct.

The work required translating operating requirements into instructions personnel could follow. I also completed more than 80% of the department's DCI-related projects while supporting daily operations.

Relevance to customer support: Clear written procedures help personnel navigate unfamiliar processes and provide a reference for later questions.

Marine Corps | User support and technical instruction

I assisted a unit help desk serving approximately 200 users, provided Windows and network support, and trained more than 210 personnel. My communications work included clarifying supported units' needs, assessing feasibility, coordinating equipment and personnel, and sustaining service in deployed environments.

Across the documented broader award period, I processed more than 150 maintenance and service requests through GCSS-MC for equipment valued above $8.6 million. These responsibilities involved maintenance and supply coordination, detailed service records, and equipment accountability.

Relevance to customer support: The experience supports my approach to technical instruction, request tracking, customer communication, and coordinated follow-through.
